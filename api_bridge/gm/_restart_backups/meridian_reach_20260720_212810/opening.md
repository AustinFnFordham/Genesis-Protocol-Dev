The Meridian Reach had learned long ago not to wait for rescue.

Its settlements were scattered across wide distances, joined by old trade lanes, volunteer relays, and the stubborn belief that a neighbor in need should never have to ask twice. The Republic had brought law and learning to the frontier, but it was the people of the Reach who kept its farms alive, its ships moving, and its isolated worlds speaking to one another through the dark.

The Jedi who came here had understood that.

They did not arrive as generals or judges. They walked the fields, repaired moisture pumps, listened to old grievances, and slept beneath the same leaking roofs as the people they served. In time, the frontier gave them a name of its own.

The Walking Cloaks.

Aeleron Do'ordo had grown beneath the shadow of that tradition.

Now, for the first time, no master stood beside him.

Vael Taron had disappeared somewhere within the Reach, leaving behind no body, no final message, and no answer beyond the faint impression Aero had felt after four days of meditation: distant, obscured, but not gone.

The Council had given Aero a title.

Knight.

The word still felt heavier than the lightsaber at his side.

His first command was supposed to be simple.

A Republic shuttle carried him toward the outer reaches of the sector with three young operatives he had never met before and six engineers whose equipment crates filled most of the aft compartment. Their destination was an abandoned beacon relay — one of the old frontier links that had gone silent after years of neglect.

Restore the station. Confirm the signal. Bring everyone home.

Routine work.

The kind of work the Walking Cloaks had once done without ceremony.

Beyond the viewport, the stars stretched across the black, and somewhere ahead of them the Meridian Reach waited — quiet, familiar, and still holding the last trace of Aero's missing master.

The shuttle's cabin hums with recycled air and the low harmonics of the hyperdrive. Dax Ferren fills the cockpit doorway with one boot on the frame, halfway through a story nobody asked for. Besk Ollor's six engineers have colonized the aft compartment, their crates strapped in tight rows. And across the cabin sit the three operatives the Republic assigned to Aero's first command — Senn Varga checking his gear for the fourth time, Ilo Reeth watching the young Knight without quite watching him, Maza Ketrel counting ration packs against a manifest.

None of them have served together before. All of them know who his master was.
